# Viraaasat Research 33 — Story 01 Selection & Development
**Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Select and develop Viraaasat's first publication topic into a realistic, evidence-led story brief.

## Executive decision

### Recommended Story 01: **The Water Beneath India**
**Working editorial question:** *What happens to a country that depends on water it cannot see?*

Focus: India's groundwater—how it is replenished, extracted, used, managed, and why the picture varies dramatically between places. This is **not** a national doom narrative. The story should investigate both pressure and nuance: groundwater is a crucial everyday resource, national averages can conceal local stress, and assessment categories are not interchangeable with individual wells or aquifers.

The 2025 Central Ground Water Board assessment reported 448.52 BCM annual recharge, 407.75 BCM annual extractable resource, 247.22 BCM annual extraction, and 60.63% national stage of extraction. These national values should not be used as a simplistic verdict: the assessment is made at administrative assessment-unit level and regional conditions vary. (PIB, 29 Jan 2026.)

**Why this is the strongest first story:** it is deeply Indian yet globally legible; connects everyday life, agriculture, cities, geology, climate, infrastructure, and policy; supports visual and interactive storytelling; has official data and clear sources; and offers a manageable narrative if we limit the scope and avoid trying to explain every water issue in India.

### Interpretation

- **Groundwater** leads because of breadth, official data access, strong visual metaphors, and cross-domain storytelling—but it must be geographically and narratively narrowed.
- **Heat** is an excellent alternate first story and may be more urgent, but it needs careful attribution, current climate evidence, and ideally field reporting. Vulnerability cannot be reduced to a temperature map.
- **Rail** is the most production-feasible and visually powerful alternative, with extensive official statistical material; it risks becoming a familiar “India's railways are huge” spectacle unless centered on a specific question.
- **Urban inequality** can be compelling, but requires careful case selection, local reporting, comparisons with compatible definitions, and ethical handling of communities.
- **Craft** offers excellent human photography and sensory storytelling, but needs direct access, voices, and compensation/permission-aware reporting to avoid romanticizing labor.
- **India 2040** is a speculative flagship, but should not be the debut: assumptions and expert input would make the work more demanding and easier to mistake for prediction.

## 4. Why groundwater works as Story 01

### Editorial promise
Make a hidden system visible. Readers often encounter water as taps, wells, tanks, irrigation, shortages, tanker queues, or rainfall. Groundwater connects these experiences but is largely invisible.

### The central tension
Groundwater is simultaneously:
- an essential reserve and a resource under pressure in some areas;
- replenished through rainfall and other pathways, but not at the same rate or in the same places as it is used;
- locally governed and measured through complex administrative assessments;
- indispensable for agriculture, households, and some industries;
- affected by geography, geology, infrastructure, crop choices, energy, and policy.

Avoid framing the entire country as either “running out of water” or “water-secure.” The evidence should show regional differences, mechanisms, and uncertainty.

### Potential story title options
- **The Water Beneath India** (recommended working title)
- **India's Invisible Water**
- **Below the Surface**
- **The Ground That Gives**
- **A Country Drawing From Below**

No title is locked yet.

## 5. Proposed narrative arc

This is an initial blueprint, to be refined after source review and reporting.

### Opening — A familiar resource, an unseen system
Start with a concrete scene: a hand pump, a borewell switch, an irrigation field, a rooftop tank being filled, or water arriving by tanker. Do not stage or fabricate a scene. Use a documented location and obtain permission if filming/photographing.

### Chapter 1 — Beneath the surface
Explain aquifers and groundwater simply, using a clear cross-section illustration. Avoid treating groundwater as a single underground lake. Explain different aquifer types and why geology matters.

### Chapter 2 — The water cycle is not a refill button
Show recharge and extraction. Explain that recharge estimates, extractable resources, and actual extraction are different quantities. Introduce the national picture carefully, then explain why national averages can hide local conditions.

### Chapter 3 — India, in uneven water
Interactive map of assessment units or state-level data. Let readers compare selected areas. Ensure the legend, classification, date, and unit are clear. Provide table/list alternatives and source notes.

### Chapter 4 — What draws water out?
Explain key uses—irrigation, domestic, industry—with evidence. Avoid assuming one factor is universally dominant. Where relevant, examine electricity, crop patterns, water-intensive agriculture, urban growth, and local rules as a set of interacting drivers.

### Chapter 5 — Pressure and possibility
Show places with different assessment statuses, management approaches, and responses. Treat conservation interventions with evidence; do not portray one technique as universally effective. Consider recharge, demand management, irrigation, crop change, community governance, measurement, and wastewater reuse only where sources and reporting support them.

### Closing — What we cannot see still shapes how we live
Synthesize: groundwater isn't an abstract resource; it's infrastructure, ecology, geology, and choices. Leave readers with a better mental model, not a single simplistic call to action.

## 6. Interactive / visual opportunities

Keep the first story focused. Aim for 3–4 meaningful visual modules.

1. **Aquifer cross-section** — scroll-revealed diagram showing rainfall, infiltration, recharge, aquifer, well/borewell, pumping, and water table. Static fallback required.
2. **Groundwater map** — national or selected-state map based on CGWB assessment-unit data. Avoid a national map if data quality, boundary reconciliation, or labels cannot be handled reliably.
3. **Extraction vs. replenishment explainer** — visual comparison of annual recharge, extractable resource, and extraction. Explicitly label national values and assessment year.
4. **Place-based case study** — one location selected only after evidence and access review; this may be the strongest emotional anchor.

Potential motion: surface fades away to reveal a below-ground section; rainfall enters the ground; the water table changes; data points appear. Avoid theatrical disaster animation or implying a single uniform aquifer.

## 7. Evidence and source plan

### Primary/official starting sources
- Central Ground Water Board (CGWB): Dynamic Ground Water Resources Assessment and state/assessment-unit tables.
- CGWB: Groundwater quality reports and monitoring data, where relevant.
- Ministry of Jal Shakti / PIB release of assessment findings.
- India-WRIS and relevant state water departments, after access/data compatibility checks.
- MoSPI and other official sources for agriculture, consumption, household, and labor context if required.

### Supporting research
- World Bank, academic hydrogeology and water-management studies, peer-reviewed work.
- CEEW and other specialist institutions, but treat their interpretations as secondary research and inspect their methods.
- Local reporting and field interviews for grounded human context.

### Data-quality checklist
- Confirm the exact assessment year and report version.
- Distinguish dynamic resources from static aquifer storage and other groundwater concepts.
- Verify the administrative unit definitions and boundaries.
- Compare like-for-like years and definitions only.
- Do not infer household well status from a block/taluka assessment.
- State limitations and data gaps.
- Preserve methodology, downloads, and analysis notes.

## 8. Case-study selection: do not choose yet

A local case study could anchor the narrative, but the team should not choose it solely because it is familiar or photogenic.

Shortlist potential case-study areas using:
- a clear, documented groundwater story;
- publicly accessible data at a useful spatial level;
- contrasting conditions (pressure, safe status, management, or geography);
- opportunity for interviews/field access;
- ability to photograph/record ethically;
- travel and production budget;
- safety and local context;
- manageable scope.

**Nagpur/Maharashtra can be explored as one candidate region, but local familiarity alone should not determine selection.** Compare at least 2–3 candidate locations before committing.

## 9. Scope guardrails

### In scope
- Groundwater basics.
- Recharge and extraction, with definitions.
- Regional differences.
- Major uses.
- Drivers and trade-offs supported by evidence.
- One place-based case study if feasible.
- Practical interventions evaluated with context.

### Out of scope for Story 01
- Every Indian water problem (surface water, dams, rivers, floods, drinking-water schemes, sanitation, ocean water).
- A full national water policy history.
- Exhaustive state-by-state analysis.
- A definitive prediction of “when India runs out of water.”
- A universal solution package.
- Unverified household stories or dramatic imagery.

- Choose location/case study.

### Phase D — Build
- Create story page and core scenes.
- Implement cross-section, map/visualization, and case study.
- Add responsive and reduced-motion alternatives.

- Peer review major claims.
- Fact-check charts/maps.
- Verify photo rights and consent.
- Mobile, keyboard, performance, and accessibility QA.
- Editorial sign-off.

For the first story, limit the number of custom interactions. The story should be ambitious in its clarity and emotional impact, not in the number of components.

## 11. Story 01 draft brief

**Story ID:** VA-001  
**Working title:** The Water Beneath India  
**Story format:** Immersive explainer / visual story  
**Primary topic:** Geography & Environment  
**Secondary topics:** Society, Agriculture, Infrastructure, Public Policy  
**Central question:** What happens to a country that depends on water it cannot see?  
**Audience:** Curious readers who want a deeper understanding of India, without requiring prior hydrology knowledge.  
**Audience takeaway:** Groundwater is not a single national reserve; it is a complex, unevenly replenished resource whose use and condition vary by place. Understanding that variation changes how we think about water.  
**Narrative device:** Surface → underground → national picture → regional contrast → lived experience → possible responses.  
**Hero visual:** A real, verified water-use scene transitioning into a scientifically accurate aquifer cross-section.  
**Core interaction:** A simple, accessible cross-section or regional data comparison.  
**Reporting requirement:** At least one place-based reporting angle if access and time allow; otherwise explicitly label the story as desk-researched and do not imply field reporting.  
**Risk level:** Elevated due to environmental, policy, and livelihood implications; claims require careful evidence and context.  
**Launch ambition:** Tier II, but keep a tightly scoped production plan.

### Success criteria
- Reader can explain what groundwater is and how it is replenished/extracted.
- Reader understands why national averages conceal regional differences.
- Reader can distinguish recharge, extractable resource, and extraction.
- The story has a human context, not only charts.
- All key claims are sourced and all visuals accurately labeled.
- It works on mobile and without motion.

## 12. Backup first-story options

### Option B — Living in a Hotter India
Strong urgency, maps, weather/climate data, exposure, and adaptation. Needs careful distinction between heat hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and health impact. Excellent candidate for a future summer-season story.

### Option C — The Country Connected by Rail
Strong visual identity and official data availability. A map-first interactive journey can be powerful. Needs a specific question beyond “India has a large railway network,” such as how the network shapes distance, time, and mobility, and avoid making the story merely celebratory.

### Option D — Two Cities, One City
Strong human story potential around unequal access to housing, water, mobility, heat, or public space. Requires selecting two compatible neighborhoods/cases, careful comparisons, and field reporting. Best as a locally reported flagship rather than a broad national generalization.
