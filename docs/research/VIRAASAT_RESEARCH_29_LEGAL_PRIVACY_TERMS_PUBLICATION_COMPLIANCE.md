# Viraaasat Research 29
## Legal, Privacy, Terms of Use & Publication Compliance for an Indian Digital Editorial Platform
**Research date:** 22 November 2026  
**Scope:** Viraaasat.in — a small, India-focused digital storytelling and editorial publication  
**Status:** Practical research and risk checklist, not legal advice

---

## Executive Summary

Viraaasat should launch with a small but clear legal and reader-policy foundation. The site should not collect unnecessary personal information, should have an easy way to report factual errors and copyright concerns, should document the rights behind every media asset, and should use careful editorial procedures for allegations, sensitive personal information, reader submissions, and contested claims.

**Priority launch documents and routes:**
1. Privacy Policy
2. Terms of Use
3. Editorial Standards / Evidence & Corrections Policy
4. Copyright, Permissions & Takedown Contact
5. Contact page (including an editorial contact route)
6. Reader-submission terms and consent, if submissions are enabled
7. A documented internal response process for privacy requests, corrections, complaints, and rights claims

A website policy is not a substitute for a safe data architecture or responsible reporting. Legal questions involving Viraaasat's publication classification, ownership structure, data processing, contentious reporting, and funding should be reviewed by a qualified Indian lawyer.

## 1. Legal landscape: what needs attention

### 1.1 Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025

India's DPDP Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 have a phased commencement schedule. The Gazette notifications establish different commencement dates for different provisions.

As of this research date, the commencement schedule is:

| Commencement | What begins | Viraaasat relevance |
|---|---|---|
| 13 Nov 2025 | Initial provisions, definitions, Data Protection Board framework and other listed sections/rules | The framework and institutions are in place; read the official instruments |
| 13 Nov 2026 (one year after publication) | Consent Manager-related provisions, including Rule 4 and corresponding listed Act provisions | Not likely a core launch feature for a simple publication, but avoid assuming every provision is already active |
| 13 May 2027 (18 months after publication) | Major substantive duties and rules, including notice, processing obligations, security safeguards, breach intimation, erasure, contact information, consent, rights, and related provisions | Build privacy-by-design now rather than waiting for the full commencement date |

These dates are computed from the Gazette publication date (13 November 2025) and the notified one-year/eighteen-month periods. Confirm current official notifications and amendments before implementation.

**Viraaasat actions:**
- Inventory every item of personal data collected and why.
- Avoid collecting data without a clear purpose.
- Make reader-facing privacy explanations clear and plain-language.
- Define access, retention, deletion, security, and response procedures.
- Use privacy-friendly analytics and minimize third-party scripts.
- Review privacy requirements before introducing forms, newsletter signup, user accounts, uploads, or personalization.

Official sources:

### 1.2 IT Rules, 2021 — digital news/current affairs publishers

Part III of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 applies to publishers of news and current affairs content and online curated content in circumstances described by Rule 8. The rules include a Code of Ethics and a grievance framework. The rules describe news and current affairs broadly, including noteworthy analysis and information about recent socio-political, economic, or cultural events.

Viraaasat is a planned editorial publication about India. Whether particular content, the publication's overall activity, and its legal/operational structure bring it within the applicable requirements should be reviewed by Indian counsel. **Do not assume that being small, independent, a website, or not calling itself a news outlet automatically exempts it.**

The Ministry of Information & Broadcasting says its Digital Media Division administers Part III and describes the three-tier grievance framework. Its public notice states that there is no prior-registration requirement in the notice's context, while Rule 18 addresses furnishing specified information; verify the current rule and applicability with counsel.

**Action:** before launch, obtain a legal applicability review for Viraaasat's intended editorial scope and operating model, including whether Rule 18 information furnishing, grievance mechanisms, or other Part III requirements apply.

Official sources:

- IT Rules 2021 (MeitY copy): https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/IT-Intermediary-Rules-2021-updated-on-28.10.2022.pdf

### 1.3 Copyright Act, 1957

Section 52 contains specified exceptions, including fair dealing for criticism or review and reporting current events/current affairs, among other limited categories. It is **not a blanket “fair use” permission**, nor does it authorize copying arbitrary amounts of third-party material simply because Viraaasat is editorial, educational, nonprofit, or credits the source.

**Operational rule:** rights clearance remains the default for images, videos, music, maps, archival material, illustrations, and fonts. If relying on an exception, document the legal and editorial reasoning and obtain review for material or high-risk uses.

Official source:
- Copyright Act (Government copyright office): https://copyright.gov.in/documents/copyright_act_1957.pdf
- Section 52 text: https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/copyright-act-1957/section-52-2

### 1.4 Defamation, privacy, reputation, and sensitive reporting

No short disclaimer can reliably neutralize a defamatory or privacy-invasive publication. Claims about identifiable individuals or organizations need a careful factual basis, evidence, attribution, context, and appropriate review. Allegations, serious wrongdoing, disputed claims, and potentially harmful personal information require elevated scrutiny.

For stories involving allegations or serious criticism:
- Separate facts, inference, opinion, and allegations.
- Keep source records and original documents.
- Seek corroboration where appropriate.
- Give a subject a meaningful opportunity to respond when fair and practicable.
- Record the response or non-response accurately.
- Avoid implying guilt from accusation or investigation alone.
- Review headlines, captions, graphics, and social posts—not only the article body.
- Consider privacy, safety, foreseeable harm, and necessity.
- Escalate to a qualified editor/lawyer when the potential harm or legal exposure is significant.

### 1.5 Press Council of India Norms of Journalistic Conduct

The Press Council's Norms of Journalistic Conduct (2022) are a useful ethical reference for accuracy, fairness, privacy, communal reporting, and other journalistic standards. Their legal applicability to Viraaasat's particular form should not be assumed; use them as an editorial reference and ask counsel about any formal applicability.

Source:
- Press Council of India, Norms of Journalistic Conduct, 2022: https://presscouncil.nic.in/WriteReadData/Pdf/Norms2022.pdf

## 2. Launch policy package

### 2.1 Privacy Policy

A privacy policy should reflect the **actual systems in use**, not a generic template copied from another site.

Recommended sections:
1. Who operates Viraaasat (publication/team contact details; legal entity details if applicable)
2. What data is collected
   - Basic server logs/technical data
   - Newsletter email address, if enabled
   - Form submissions and voluntary contact information
   - Cookies/local storage, if actually used
3. Why each data item is collected
4. Services and processors involved (hosting, analytics, newsletter, form tools)
5. Whether information is shared and under what circumstances
6. Retention periods or criteria
7. Security and access practices described accurately
8. Reader choices and requests
9. Contact for privacy questions
10. Policy version/date and how changes will be communicated

**Recommended Viraaasat approach:**
- Collect only what is necessary.
- No accounts or profiling at launch.
- No session recording or invasive heatmaps.
- Use privacy-conscious analytics with a carefully reviewed configuration.
- Avoid third-party embeds that silently collect data; prefer click-to-load.
- Newsletter signup should collect only email and any genuinely necessary optional field.
- Make unsubscribe easy and promptly honor it.
- Don't promise absolute security, anonymity, or deletion from every backup unless that promise can be fulfilled.

### 2.2 Terms of Use

Keep the terms readable. Include:
- What Viraaasat is and what its content is intended to provide
- Editorial content is for information and general understanding, not individualized professional advice
- Content may change and corrections may be made
- Ownership/licensing of Viraaasat's original material
- Permitted personal use, sharing links, and citation
- Prohibited scraping, copying substantial material, unauthorized commercial reuse, malicious activity, and abuse of site infrastructure
- Third-party links and embedded material
- Contact for rights and legal concerns
- Governing law and jurisdiction only after legal review; do not invent an enforceable jurisdiction clause without confirming the appropriate wording

Avoid sweeping terms that purport to waive every liability or user's legal rights.

### 2.3 Editorial Standards and Corrections Policy

Publicly explain:
- Evidence expectations and source transparency
- How Viraaasat handles disputed facts and uncertainty
- Distinguishing reporting from analysis, opinion, reconstruction, and illustration
- How corrections are submitted
- How significant corrections are disclosed
- How readers can challenge missing context or misleading presentation
- AI use disclosure principles, where relevant
- Conflict-of-interest and sponsorship separation

Suggested correction approach:
- Minor typographical corrections: correct without a correction note unless meaning changes.
- Material factual corrections: add a dated, visible note explaining the correction.
- Significant changes to interpretation or framing: preserve a clear record of the update.
- Never silently alter a consequential error in a way that obscures what changed.

### 2.4 Copyright / Permissions / Takedown page

Provide a clear contact route for:
- Copyright and licensing questions
- Permission requests
- Suspected unauthorized use
- Requests concerning reader-submitted media
- Credit corrections
- Privacy or safety concerns relating to published media

Suggested intake details:
- Name/contact (optional where appropriate, but enough information to respond)
- URL of the affected story/content
- Description of the material and concern
- Claimant's relationship to the material
- Evidence or links supporting the claim
- Requested action

Do not automatically remove content solely because a request is received. Acknowledge, assess, preserve relevant records, and seek advice where necessary. Do not ignore credible claims. Set an internal response target but do not promise legal deadlines unless verified.

### 2.5 Contact page

Separate routes or subject labels help the team triage:
- Story ideas
- Corrections / factual concerns
- Permissions / copyright
- Reader feedback
- Privacy requests
- General contact

Avoid publishing personal phone numbers or personal email addresses unless the team intentionally accepts that exposure. Use a shared editorial contact inbox or controlled contact form.

## 3. Reader submissions and contributor safety

Reader submissions can include personal stories, photos, videos, documents, or allegations. Start with low-risk forms and avoid open upload functionality at launch.

Before accepting a submission, clearly explain:
- What is being requested
- Whether it may be used in a published story
- Whether the team may contact the contributor
- Whether identifying details may be published
- How attribution works
- Whether any compensation is offered (do not imply it if none is)
- How to withdraw or request changes before publication and what happens after publication
- That submitting does not guarantee publication
- How the team will protect or handle sensitive information, without promising absolute confidentiality

### Suggested submission workflow
1. Receive and acknowledge.
2. Triage for relevance, safety, privacy, and legal risk.
3. Verify facts and provenance.
4. Confirm consent/permissions and attribution preferences.
5. Obtain editorial review before use.
6. Store securely with limited access.
7. Publish only what is necessary and approved.
8. Record source, permission, and decisions in the story's editorial ledger.

**Launch recommendation:** use a simple contact/submission form without file uploads. If a story requires documents or media, create a specific, secure collection process rather than a generic upload box.

## 4. Privacy-by-design data checklist

| Data | Launch need | Recommended practice |
|---|---|---|
| Server logs | Usually technical operation/security | Confirm host retention and access |
| Analytics | Aggregate understanding | Avoid personal identifiers and sensitive profiling |
| Newsletter email | Optional reader relationship | Consent, secure provider, unsubscribe |
| Reader feedback | Editorial input | Minimize fields; allow anonymous feedback if feasible |
| Contact form | Communication | Collect only message and necessary reply details |
| Cookies | Depends on implementation | Audit; explain purpose; avoid unnecessary tracking |
| Location | Usually unnecessary | Don't collect precise location |
| Uploads | Not needed initially | Keep disabled |
| Accounts | Not needed initially | Keep disabled |
| Session replay | Not recommended | Avoid |
| Ad trackers | Not needed at launch | Avoid |

### Data inventory template

For every data flow, record:
- Data category
- Source
- Purpose
- Whether required or optional
- Provider/processor
- Storage location
- Who can access it
- Retention period
- Deletion process
- Security controls
- Applicable policy wording
- Responsible team member

## 5. Cookies, analytics, and third-party embeds

### Cookie audit
- Inventory cookies, local storage, and tracking scripts.
- Identify essential versus nonessential technologies.
- Check whether any analytics/advertising tools transmit identifiers or IP data.
- Avoid claiming “no cookies” until verified in the browser and through vendor documentation.
- Recheck when adding analytics, newsletter, video, maps, or social embeds.

### Embed policy
Prefer:
- Link previews or click-to-load embeds
- Lazy loading
- Minimal third-party services
- Clear labeling when leaving Viraaasat
- No automatic audio
- No unnecessary tracking

Potential third-party embeds (YouTube, social media, maps, etc.) should be assessed individually for data collection, loading behaviour, permissions, license/terms, and fallback accessibility.

## 6. Editorial legal-risk workflow

### Risk tiers

**Tier A — routine**
- Evergreen factual explainers
- Science/geography with strong sources
- Cultural/history stories without sensitive claims
- Public datasets with verified licensing

Standard editorial and fact-check process.

**Tier B — elevated**
- Political or policy analysis
- Contested history
- Sensitive cultural/communal matters
- Environmental disputes
- Claims about institutions or identifiable people
- Reader-submitted testimony

Require second-person review of claims, sourcing, framing, visuals, and headline.

**Tier C — high risk**
- Allegations of criminality, fraud, corruption, abuse, or misconduct
- Private individuals in sensitive contexts
- Threats to safety or vulnerable people
- Confidential material or leaked documents
- Highly disputed claims likely to cause serious harm

Pause publication until senior editorial review and, where warranted, qualified legal review. Consider whether the story is necessary, sufficiently evidenced, proportionate, and safe.

### Claim verification checklist
For each material claim:
- What exactly is being asserted?
- What source supports it?
- Is the source primary or secondary?
- Has it been independently corroborated?
- Does the source actually say what the draft claims?
- What is the date, scope, denominator, definition, and limitation?
- Is there relevant context or a counter-evidence?
- Does the subject have a meaningful response opportunity when appropriate?
- Is the claim worded proportionately to the evidence?
- Does the headline/visual preserve the same accuracy?

## 7. Copyright and media-use checklist

Before publishing each asset, verify:
- Asset ID and story
- Creator/rights holder
- Source URL or permission evidence
- License and version
- Attribution requirements
- Whether commercial use is allowed (important if Viraaasat later monetizes)
- Whether modifications/cropping are permitted
- Expiry or territorial restrictions
- Privacy/consent concerns
- Editorial relevance and authenticity
- Caption and credit
- Reviewer and date cleared

Do not treat:
- Google Images search results as a license
- Credit as permission
- “Educational” as automatic permission
- “Nonprofit” as automatic permission
- A watermark-free image as free
- AI-generated photorealism as factual documentation

If relying on Copyright Act Section 52, record the specific exception and why the use fits; ask counsel when uncertain.

## 8. Corrections, complaints, and response operations

Create a single internal issue log with separate categories:
- Factual correction
- Missing context
- Copyright/licensing
- Privacy/safety
- Technical issue
- Accessibility
- Reader feedback
- Legal notice

### Proposed triage process
1. Log receipt, date, story URL, issue category.
2. Assign one responsible person.
3. Acknowledge receipt where a reply route exists.
4. Assess urgency and potential harm.
5. Preserve relevant material and records.
6. Investigate with the appropriate reviewer.
7. Decide: no change, clarification, correction, temporary restriction, or escalation.
8. Communicate outcome where appropriate.
9. Update correction/rights log.
10. Review process if recurring.

Use internal service targets such as:
- Urgent safety/legal concern: triage as soon as practical.
- Factual correction: review within a few working days.
- Routine permission query: acknowledge within a few working days.

These are suggested team targets, not statements of statutory deadlines.

## 9. Terms that should not be used carelessly

Avoid policy statements such as:
- “We are not responsible for anything.”
- “All information is guaranteed accurate.”
- “We can publish anything submitted to us.”
- “Submitting content transfers all rights to us.”
- “We guarantee anonymity.”
- “All third-party content is used under fair use.”
- “Your data is never shared.”
- “We delete everything immediately.”

These may be inaccurate, unenforceable, or create expectations the team cannot meet.

Use plain-language, specific, operationally true statements instead.

2. **Terms of Use** — draft a plain-language baseline; legal review before launch.
3. **Editorial Standards & Corrections** — align with Research 13 and Research 25.
4. **Copyright & Permissions** — align with Research 20 and the media-rights ledger.
5. **Contact routes** — implement shared editorial inbox/contact form.
6. **Reader-submission notice** — only if submissions are enabled.
7. **Legal applicability review** — specifically assess news/current-affairs publisher obligations under the IT Rules.
8. **DPDP implementation plan** — design and implement against the notified phased timeline, with a legal/privacy professional review.

## 13. Viraaasat-specific recommendations

- Keep launch data collection minimal.
- Avoid reader accounts, open comments, and file uploads initially.
- Use a shared team contact rather than personal accounts.
- Treat political, communal, allegation-heavy, or sensitive stories as elevated/high-risk.
- Have at least one person other than the story author review high-risk claims.
- Do not assume the publication is exempt from digital news publisher requirements because it is small or independent.
- Treat policies as living documents; revise when actual services change.
- Keep policy pages accessible, linked in the footer, and written for ordinary readers.

## Bottom line

Viraaasat's legal foundation should be **quiet, clear, and credible**. Readers shouldn't have to navigate legal jargon to understand how the publication works, how their information is handled, how errors can be challenged, or how to contact the team.

The goal isn't to cover ourselves with disclaimers. It is to build a publication that can demonstrate responsible practices when it matters.
