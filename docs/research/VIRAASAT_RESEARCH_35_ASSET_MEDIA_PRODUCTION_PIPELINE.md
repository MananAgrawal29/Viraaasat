# Viraaasat Research 35 — Asset & Media Production Pipeline
**Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Mission:** Build a practical, repeatable pipeline for sourcing, creating, editing, verifying, organizing, optimizing, and publishing visual and audio assets for Viraaasat.in.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Viraaasat should not look like a collection of beautiful stock photographs. Every media asset should be specific to the story, have a traceable origin, fit the editorial purpose, and be delivered efficiently without compromising documentary integrity.

**Recommended philosophy:** documentary first, aesthetic second.

Prioritize:
- Specificity — actual places, objects, people, events, or phenomena being discussed.
- Authenticity — documentary evidence rather than staged scenes masquerading as reportage.
- Visual storytelling — every asset performs a role.
- Texture — India as lived, observed, imperfect, and layered.
- Variation — landscapes, people, details, documents, objects, data, and archive.
- Respect — especially when depicting poverty, suffering, communities, religious practices, or sensitive subjects.

A photograph should not merely look like India. It should help the reader understand this particular part of India.

This report separates sourced guidance from Viraaasat-specific recommendations.

## 2. Asset sourcing hierarchy

This is a recommended priority hierarchy, not a rule that every story must use Tier 1 media.

### Tier 1 — Original and commissioned media
- Team photography and field footage
- Commissioned photographers and videographers
- Original interviews and field recordings
- Custom illustrations
- Original maps and data visualizations

Highest editorial value and strongest potential for visual uniqueness.

### Tier 2 — Archives and primary collections
- Historical photographs
- Government records
- Institutional archives
- Maps, documents, scans, objects
- Original records

Essential for history, evidence, context, and visual authenticity.

### Tier 3 — Licensed documentary photography
- Photographers
- Photo agencies and editorial libraries
- Direct licensing arrangements

Useful for important scenes when original photography is not feasible.

### Tier 4 — Open-license photography
- Wikimedia Commons
- Creative Commons media
- Public-domain collections
- Appropriately licensed image libraries

Practical for supporting visuals, but individual rights and restrictions still need checking.

### Tier 5 — Created visual assets
- Charts and diagrams
- Maps
- Illustrations
- Reconstructed scenes (clearly labeled)
- Typographic compositions
- Designed transitions

Used when photography cannot explain the idea.

## 3. Source discovery

### Contemporary photography and video
- Unsplash — https://unsplash.com/
- Pexels — https://www.pexels.com/
- Pixabay — https://pixabay.com/

Use these for discovery, textures, supplementary imagery, and occasional visual assets. Do not allow generic stock imagery to become Viraaasat's default identity. Read the current platform license and restrictions, and verify the individual asset and context.

Unsplash's license permits broad free use subject to restrictions. Pexels allows broad use and modification under its license, but prohibits certain uses, including misleading implications of endorsement and inappropriate depictions of identifiable people. Attribution is not generally required under these platform licenses, but editorial credit is still recommended where appropriate.
Sources:
- https://unsplash.com/license
- https://www.pexels.com/license/
- https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/

### Historical, archival, and primary media
- Wikimedia Commons — https://commons.wikimedia.org/
- Smithsonian Open Access — https://www.si.edu/openaccess
- Government and institutional archives
- Museums and heritage institutions
- Newspapers, libraries, universities, collections, and research centers

Wikimedia Commons files carry individual license conditions. Smithsonian Open Access assets marked CC0 are broadly reusable, but third-party privacy, publicity, cultural sensitivity, and other restrictions may still apply. Check item-level rights and context.

### Source-specific reminders
- **Wikimedia Commons:** verify each file's license, creator, attribution instructions, and any restrictions. Different files can have different licenses.
- **Smithsonian Open Access:** confirm CC0 status on the specific asset; do not assume every Smithsonian asset is open.
- **Stock platforms:** the platform's license is not a substitute for assessing context, subject suitability, and restrictions.
- **Institutional archives:** rights may vary by record, institution, jurisdiction, and purpose.

## 4. Visual asset families

Stories should deliberately mix visual types instead of using one photographic style throughout.

### 01. Establishing images
Establish place, scale, atmosphere, and geographical context.

### 02. Human portraits
Give stories a human dimension. Avoid anonymous stock-model expressions.

### 03. Details and textures
Hands, surfaces, machinery, tools, objects, architecture, and environmental details.

### 04. Archival assets
Original records, historic photographs, manuscripts, newspapers, maps, and documents.

### 05. Data and explanatory graphics
Charts, timelines, maps, diagrams, and visual explanations.

### 06. Movement and cinematic footage
Video, environmental motion, sequences, and moving visual transitions.

A story should not be dominated by aerial shots, generic city skylines, monument shots, dramatic close-ups, images from the same photographer, or the same palette unless this is a deliberate editorial choice.

## 5. Viraaasat Asset Pipeline

### Stage 01 — Story asset brief
Before image searches begin, create a brief based on the storyboard.

For every visual need, record:
- Chapter
- Narrative purpose
- Asset type
- Required subject
- Mood (if relevant)
- Composition (wide, portrait, macro, negative space, etc.)
- Motion plan (static, pan, reveal, sequence, etc.)
- Priority (essential / useful / optional)
- Source requirements (primary, archive, commissioned, licensed)
- Mobile requirements

This prevents collecting hundreds of interesting images without knowing which ones belong in the story.

### Stage 02 — Research and shortlist
For each visual need:
1. Search using subject + place + visual type.
2. Search for the actual subject rather than generic keyword matches.
3. Look through institutional collections when historical specificity matters.
4. Save multiple candidates.
5. Compare composition, authenticity, resolution, narrative relevance, and rights.
6. Keep a shortlist rather than immediately committing.

Proposed shortlist size: 3–8 candidates for a major image, fewer for minor supporting assets.

### Stage 03 — Rights and provenance verification
Before an asset enters the final production folder, verify:
- Creator / photographer
- Original source URL
- License type
- Required attribution
- Modification permissions
- Commercial-use limitations, if relevant
- Third-party rights
- Model/property release considerations, where applicable
- Cultural sensitivity concerns
- Evidence of the rights claim

A license is not the only question. An image may be reusable but still be misleading, contextually inappropriate, or ethically problematic.

Media licensing may have restrictions on media, duration, territory, editorial vs. commercial use, and publication context. Rights-managed licenses do not transfer ownership.

### Stage 04 — Edit and create
- Select the correct frame.
- Crop and straighten.
- Correct exposure.
- Correct white balance.
- Adjust contrast.
- Refine highlights and shadows.
- Apply restrained color grading.
- Sharpen appropriately.
- Export web versions.

### Stage 05 — Optimize and integrate
- Choose format.
- Compress.
- Create responsive variants.
- Reserve dimensions/aspect ratio.
- Integrate into the story.
- Confirm the correct version is referenced in code/content.

### Stage 06 — QA and publish
- Visual quality
- Rights and attribution
- Captions and context
- Mobile composition
- Accessibility and fallback
- Performance
- Final asset ledger completion

## 6. Editing philosophy and documentary integrity

### Generally appropriate edits
- Crop
- Exposure and tonal correction
- White balance
- Modest color correction
- Sharpening
- Noise reduction
- Format conversion
- Resize and compression

### Avoid
- Adding or removing evidence-bearing objects
- Combining unrelated images to represent one real scene
- Changing environmental details
- Altering a person's appearance in a way that misrepresents them
- Using photorealistic AI-generated scenes as documentary evidence
- Making archival images appear contemporary
- Creating visual context that the source does not support

If an image is reconstructed, simulated, or illustrative, label it clearly.

### Viraaasat color treatment — recommendation
- Natural skin tones remain natural.
- Warmth should not be applied indiscriminately.
- Greens should not be artificially saturated.
- Shadows may be deep, but important detail must survive.
- Grain should be subtle and intentional.
- Avoid preset-driven uniform grading across unrelated subjects.
- Avoid excessive orange/teal cinematic grading.
- Preserve documentary image truth.

**Not every story should look the same.** The brand is the design system, framing, typography, and editorial judgment—not a filter applied to every image.

### Naming convention
Use consistent, descriptive, lowercase filenames.

```text
story-slug_ch01_establishing_01.avif

story-slug_ch02_archive_01.jpg
story-slug_ch03_map_overview.svg

```

Avoid opaque names such as:
- IMG_0934.jpg
- final-final-2.jpg
- newimage.webp
- image-edited-real-final.png

## 8. Asset ledger — critical operational tool

Every published media asset should have an entry in a single source-of-truth ledger.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Asset ID | e.g. S01-A014 |
| Story | Story slug |
| Filename | Exact published file |
| Description | What is depicted |
| Creator | Photographer / artist |
| Source | Original URL |
| License | Exact license or permission |
| Credit line | Required visible attribution |
| Download date | Date acquired |
| Original file | Path to source copy |
| Edit history | What changes were made |
| Publication location | Chapter / section |
| Verification | Person who checked it |
| Rights status | Cleared / pending / rejected |
| Notes | Restrictions or contextual caveats |

**Proposed publishing rule:** no asset with unresolved rights status enters the published build.

## 9. Visual research technique

Do not search only for the topic. Search for the image the story needs.

### Search formula
Combine:
**Subject + location + time period + medium + composition**

Examples:
- subject + documentary photographer + location
- subject + historical archive + year
- subject + aerial photograph + location
- subject + close-up + details
- subject + map + official data
- subject + field photography + reportage

### Build a visual reference board
For each story, save a compact board containing:
- Visual mood
- Lighting
- Color
- Composition
- Camera distance
- Textures
- Typography reference
- Transitions
- Data visual style

The board is a direction-setting tool, not a collection of images to copy.

## 10. Photography and video production briefs

When commissioning or shooting ourselves, avoid vague instructions like “take cinematic photos.” Send a brief.

### Sample field photography brief (illustrative)
**Purpose:** document the subject truthfully while capturing environmental context, human detail, and moments that support the narrative.

**Shot list:**
- 2–3 establishing frames
- 3–5 environmental/context frames
- 4–6 detail frames
- 2–4 human moments or portraits (only with appropriate consent)
- 1–2 sequences showing process or movement
- Optional contrasting scenes

**Composition:**
- Landscape frames for immersive sections
- Portraits for human focus
- Negative space where text overlays may be used
- Vertical compositions for mobile and social
- Avoid forcing the subject into a cinematic cliché

**Deliverables:**
- Original high-resolution files
- Edited selects
- Captions and factual context
- Creator name
- Location/date where appropriate and safe
- Rights and permissions documentation

### Video production principles
- Capture short clips with a clear purpose.
- Capture more than one framing option.
- Capture static shots and movement shots.
- Record ambient sound separately where possible.
- Avoid camera movement unless it helps.
- Hold shots long enough to edit.
- Capture landscape and portrait where the shot merits both.
- Don't rely on slow-motion as a substitute for storytelling.
- Capture natural environmental audio, but don't manufacture sound to imply an event.

## 11. Audio asset workflow

Audio is optional for every story.

| Asset | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Ambient field recording | Place and atmosphere |
| Interview / voice | Human perspective and testimony |
| Sound detail | A specific action or mechanism |
| Music | Emotional framing, if appropriate |
| Designed sound | Explain transitions or concepts |

Rules:
- Never autoplay audio.
- Audio is opt-in.
- Provide mute/pause controls.
- Use audio only when it adds something unavailable visually or textually.
- Don't fabricate archival sound.
- Don't use music to manipulate a story's emotional interpretation.
- Track the license of each music and sound asset.
- Provide transcripts where meaningful.

Audio rights can be distinct for composition, recording, performance, and particular uses. Research 15 remains the audio-specific reference.

## 12. Optimization and web delivery

Images can represent a large part of page weight. Modern web guidance recommends modern formats, responsive sizing, explicit dimensions, and lazy loading for below-the-fold imagery. The primary above-the-fold image should not be lazy-loaded.

### Recommended starting formats

| Asset | Recommended starting format |
|---|---|
| Photography | AVIF or WebP |
| Photo compatibility fallback | JPEG |
| Transparent graphics | WebP or PNG |
| Logos / icons | SVG |
| Charts / diagrams | SVG |
| Short loop video | WebM or MP4 |
| Audio | WebM/Opus or MP3 fallback, depending on compatibility |
| Animated GIF | Avoid for cinematic loops; use video |

AVIF and WebP are strong photographic formats; SVG suits vector graphics. For longer animations, video generally delivers more efficiently than GIF.

### Practical export targets (Viraaasat proposal—not universal limits)

| Asset | Target range |
|---|---:|
| Small supporting image | 80–250 KB |
| Editorial image | 150–450 KB |
| Large immersive image | 300–800 KB |
| Hero image | Ideally 400–900 KB |
| Small loop video | 1–4 MB if possible |
| Heavy cinematic video | Load on demand and carefully control |

Quality matters more than rigid file-size quotas. Images with fine texture may need higher quality settings than flat graphics.

### Image variants
For a major photograph, consider:
- Mobile
- Tablet
- Desktop
- Full-resolution archival master (not delivered directly)

The browser should download the appropriate variant rather than a 4K image for a 390px phone.

### Loading policy
- Hero / LCP image: prioritized, not lazy loaded.
- Below-the-fold imagery: lazy load.
- Reserve image dimensions or aspect ratio.
- Use responsive `srcset` / `sizes`.
- Lazy-load videos and heavy interactive embeds.
- Use poster frames for videos.
- Avoid unnecessary preloads.
- Don't preload every cinematic asset.

These practices reduce unnecessary downloads and layout shifts.

## 15. Mistakes to avoid

1. **Stock-image storytelling:** images match mood but not the actual story.
2. **Rights ambiguity:** downloading first, figuring out the license later.
3. **Aesthetic homogenization:** applying the same grade, crop, motion, and style to every story.
4. **Asset hoarding:** collecting media without a storyboard or visual purpose.
5. **Untracked attribution:** losing creator or license information when files move.
6. **Over-editing:** turning documentary material into inaccurate representation.
7. **Desktop-only imagery:** wide compositions that fail on mobile or leave no room for text.
8. **Heavy media before content:** making readers wait for large media before the story begins.
9. **Poor version control:** editing an export but not knowing which file the story uses.
10. **No archive master:** losing the original file or source evidence.

## 16. Publication QA checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- [ ] Every image has an editorial purpose.
- [ ] All assets have verified sources.
- [ ] Licensing and attribution checked.
- [ ] Captions are factually accurate.
- [ ] Sensitive people/subjects reviewed.
- [ ] Documentary vs archive vs illustration labels applied.
- [ ] No misleading editing or reconstruction.
- [ ] Desktop crops checked.
- [ ] Mobile crops checked.
- [ ] Image quality and compression checked.
- [ ] Hero loading prioritized.
- [ ] Below-fold assets lazy-loaded appropriately.
- [ ] Dimensions/aspect ratios reserved.
- [ ] Video poster and fallback checked.
- [ ] Audio opt-in and controls tested.
- [ ] Alt text / text alternatives checked.
- [ ] Reduced-motion experience checked.
- [ ] Rights ledger completed.
- [ ] Unused and duplicate assets removed.
- [ ] Final assets match approved storyboard.

## 17. Final recommendations

### Adopt immediately
1. A per-story asset brief based on the storyboard.
2. A shared visual reference board.
3. A single asset ledger.
4. Clear naming conventions.
5. Separate raw, selects, edits, and exports.
6. Rights verification before final integration.
7. Responsive image exports.
8. A story-level media QA pass before release.

### Keep lightweight
- No enterprise DAM at this stage.
- No complicated approval software.
- No paid stock subscription unless a story genuinely requires it.
- No heavy production workflow for assets that do not materially improve the story.

### Editorial north star
**Every visual is chosen for what it contributes to understanding—not simply for how beautiful it looks.**

A visual can be beautiful, emotional, cinematic, and technically impressive. But if it doesn't accurately represent the subject or help the reader understand it, it doesn't belong.
