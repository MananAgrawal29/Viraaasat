# Viraaasat Research 19
## AI, Editorial Automation & Responsible AI Workflow

**Mission:** Define how Viraaasat uses AI across research, writing, coding, design exploration, data analysis, visuals, audio and publishing—saving time while protecting evidence, originality, reader trust and editorial accountability.

**Research date:** November 2026. This is a project policy proposal grounded in published newsroom AI guidance and research, not legal advice. Policies from other newsrooms are examples to learn from, not automatically binding rules for Viraaasat.

---

## Executive summary

AI should be a **research assistant, thinking partner, workflow accelerator and technical copilot—not the editorial authority or the source of truth.**

Recommended principles:

1. Humans own all published stories and decisions.
2. AI may help brainstorm, organize, summarize provided material, explain concepts, transcribe, translate drafts, create coding scaffolds, troubleshoot and critique.
3. AI-generated factual claims, citations, quotations, statistics, dates, names and source details are unverified until checked against reliable sources.
4. AI cannot invent reporting, interviews, eyewitness accounts, quotations, field observations, documents or evidence.
5. No AI-generated or materially AI-altered documentary photography, video, audio or realistic depiction of real events/people/places presented as evidence.
6. Generative imagery/audio/video may be used for clearly labeled illustrative or conceptual work only, subject to story-specific review. For factual stories, prefer authentic images, diagrams, maps, data graphics or visibly non-photorealistic illustrations.
7. AI must not receive confidential, personal, sensitive, embargoed or restricted information unless the tool, account, data handling and permission are explicitly approved.
8. AI coding output must be reviewed, tested, security-checked and understood before merging.
9. Material AI use that affects published content or evidence should be disclosed clearly and proportionately.
10. Keep a simple AI use ledger for high-impact tasks; don't document every trivial spelling suggestion.

## 1. Why AI needs a policy at Viraaasat

The team's likely AI use spans:
- Research planning and discovery
- Source finding and document summarization
- Fact-checking support
- Narrative outline and structure critique
- Writing and copy editing
- Translation and multilingual adaptation
- Code generation, debugging and tests
- Design exploration and visual critique
- Data cleaning, calculations and chart prototyping
- Image, audio and video concepts
- Publishing workflow and distribution

These uses have different risk profiles. A code assistant suggesting a CSS layout is not equivalent to an AI generating an image that appears to document a real protest.

The policy should be risk-based: **the more AI affects factual claims, evidence, audience interpretation or real-world representation, the more stringent the human review and transparency.**

## 2. What newsroom guidance suggests

Published newsroom policies share recurring themes:

- Human editorial accountability remains.
- AI output is unverified source material, not a replacement for reporting or verification.
- Accuracy, fairness, context and transparency remain essential.
- Sensitive/confidential material should not be pasted into unapproved AI services.
- AI-generated or manipulated visual material requires extra scrutiny and clear labeling when used.
- Editorial uses need ongoing review as tools and risks change.

The Associated Press' updated July 2026 guidance describes AI as assisting specific tasks such as early research, document summaries, transcription, translation, headline suggestions and software development, while retaining journalist review and editorial accountability. It prohibits generative AI from creating, altering or enhancing news photography. Reuters says AI output must be independently verified, human accountability remains, and its visual journalism does not use generative AI to create or enhance imagery. BBC guidance distinguishes supporting editorial processes from directly creating factual journalism, emphasizes oversight, and requires careful review of AI transcription/translation. The Thomson Reuters Foundation's practical guide recommends inventorying tools, identifying risks and continuously monitoring use.

These policies do not establish a universal standard for every publication; they demonstrate how responsible use can be framed. (Sources 1–5)

## 3. Viraaasat's AI philosophy

> AI may help us work faster, think wider, find patterns, prototype, question assumptions and improve our craft. It may never replace our responsibility to know what is true, decide what matters, represent people fairly or stand behind what we publish.

AI is a tool. The editorial voice, judgment and responsibility belong to the humans building Viraaasat.

## 4. AI use matrix

### Green — routinely permitted, with normal human review

| Task | Acceptable use |
|---|---|
| Brainstorming | Generate questions, topic angles, possible story structures |
| Research planning | Create search terms, suggest sources to locate |
| Summarization | Summarize documents provided, then check against originals |
| Writing support | Grammar, clarity, sentence alternatives, outline critique |
| Coding | Components, scaffolds, debugging, tests, documentation |
| Design | Moodboard descriptions, critique, alternative layouts, design reasoning |
| Data analysis | Explain formulas, propose methods, help write analysis code |
| Transcription | Draft transcripts from recordings, followed by accuracy checks |
| Translation draft | Draft language versions for qualified human review |
| QA | Generate test cases, accessibility checklists, edge-case scenarios |

### Yellow — permitted with stronger review and clear limits

| Task | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Drafting factual prose | Every claim, quote, number and framing independently checked |
| Document synthesis | Trace all important statements to original documents |
| Historical analysis | Separate evidence, interpretation and uncertainty; expert/source review |
| Sensitive-topic research | Check bias, omissions and representation; use independent review |
| Data cleaning | Validate assumptions, transformations, sample and output |
| Headline/SEO suggestions | Reject sensationalism and misleading simplification |
| AI-assisted chart/graphic | Check data, scale, labels, visual integrity and reader interpretation |
| AI-assisted translation/adaptation | Native/fluent reviewer, terminology and claim verification |
| AI-generated illustrations | Clearly label; ensure it can't be mistaken for documentary evidence |
| Audio narration | Human approval of pronunciation, voice identity, tone and rights; synthetic voice disclosed when material |

### Red — prohibited for Viraaasat

- Fabricating sources, interviews, quotations, statistics, observations or evidence.
- Publishing AI hallucinations without verification.
- Using AI-generated photorealistic depictions of real people, places or events as documentary evidence.
- Altering documentary photographs, videos or audio in ways that change their factual meaning or misrepresent reality.
- Generating a quote, testimony or voice and attributing it to a real person.
- Replacing human editorial judgment on controversial or sensitive stories with AI scoring or automated recommendations.
- Publishing fully AI-written factual stories without meaningful human authorship, sourcing, rewriting and review.
- Using AI to infer sensitive reader characteristics or target readers based on sensitive traits.
- Uploading confidential or sensitive material to unapproved AI services.
- Allowing an AI agent to publish, commit, merge, deploy, delete data or alter production without explicit human authorization and review.

## 5. Research workflow: AI can help discover, but never be the citation

### Safe research process

1. **Human defines the question.** What are we trying to establish? What could change the answer?
2. **AI helps plan.** Generate search queries, source categories, competing hypotheses and gaps.
3. **Find primary sources.** Official datasets, laws, original research, archives, transcripts, records, direct statements.
4. **AI summarizes material.** Use only as a navigational aid; retain links and source files.
5. **Human reads the original.** Verify critical claims in the actual source, not the AI summary.
6. **Cross-check.** Important claims should be corroborated with independent sources where possible.
7. **Build the claim ledger.** Each claim gets a source, evidence note, status and limitations.
8. **Draft and review.** AI may critique structure, but the story's analysis and decisions remain human-led.
9. **Fact-check.** Independently verify every published fact, date, number, name, quotation and source.
10. **Editorial approval.** Humans decide whether the story is fair, clear, contextualized and ready.

### A key rule

**Never cite an AI answer as a source.** Cite the primary/secondary source the claim is based on. If AI suggests a source, locate and verify the source yourself; don't trust a generated title, URL or citation.

## 6. AI-assisted writing workflow

### What AI is good at
- Turning a rough outline into a structure to discuss
- Identifying repetition or unclear transitions
- Suggesting alternate phrasings
- Highlighting possible missing context or counterarguments
- Simplifying difficult technical explanations
- Generating questions for an editor to consider
- Checking consistency of terminology and tone

### What humans must own
- Narrative framing
- Story angle and significance
- Interpretation and conclusions
- Choice of evidence
- Fairness, nuance and context
- Voice and originality
- Publication decisions

### Drafting rule
If AI drafts factual prose, it is **not a publishable draft until a human author has checked every factual claim against the source ledger, rewritten/edited it with intent, and accepted responsibility for the final version.**

Don't outsource the story's voice to a generic model. Viraaasat's editorial voice must emerge from its writers, observations, choices and evidence.

## 7. AI and source verification

AI may help spot inconsistencies, generate a verification checklist or identify questions—but it cannot independently verify truth.

### Claim verification table

| Claim type | Required human check |
|---|---|
| Number/statistic | Original dataset/report, definition, denominator, timeframe, method |
| Quote | Original transcript/document/recording, wording and attribution |
| Historical fact | Credible primary record and specialist sources where needed |
| Legal/policy status | Current official text and stage/status |
| Allegation | Evidence, attribution, response, independent corroboration and legal/editorial review |
| Image/video | Original source, date/location, provenance, context, reverse-search/metadata where helpful |
| AI-generated citation | Locate the actual source; verify its title, author, date and claim |
| Translation | Original wording and qualified reviewer checks nuance |

For sensitive or disputed claims, use a second reviewer. If verification fails, qualify, remove or postpone the claim.

## 8. AI coding and development workflow

AI coding assistance can save substantial time—but generated code is untrusted code until reviewed.

### Recommended pipeline

```text
Human-defined task
       ↓
AI proposes plan / implementation
       ↓
Developer reviews code and assumptions
       ↓
Run type checks + lint + tests
       ↓
Review diff, security and dependencies
       ↓
Manual UI / responsive / accessibility test
       ↓
Human approves changes
       ↓
Commit / merge / deploy (human controlled)
```

### AI coding guardrails

- Read and understand the changes.
- Never merge code blindly.
- Review package additions and install scripts.
- Check secrets, authentication and authorization.
- Validate inputs and external URLs.
- Review accessibility and responsive behavior.
- Run tests and inspect failure output.
- Don't let AI change project architecture without discussion.
- Avoid enormous, unreviewable AI-generated diffs.
- Do not grant agents unrestricted production access.

For Viraaasat, the existing team Git rules remain in effect: AI may inspect and edit files, run checks and tests, but must not commit, push, merge, rebase, reset, cherry-pick, rewrite history or stage files without explicit authorization.

## 9. AI for design, visuals and motion

### Good uses
- Visual concept exploration
- Design critique against the locked brand
- Layout alternatives
- Interaction/motion brainstorming
- Storyboard planning
- Placeholder illustrations for internal prototyping
- Accessibility and contrast review
- Responsive design critique

### Visual integrity policy

For Viraaasat's factual stories:

**AI-generated visuals must never be presented as authentic documentary evidence.**

The preferred order for factual storytelling:
1. Original documentary photography/video/audio with rights and provenance.
2. Authentic archival material with verified attribution and context.
3. Maps, diagrams, data visualization and visual explanation.
4. Clearly labeled non-photorealistic illustration.
5. Clearly labeled conceptual AI illustration only when editorially justified and not misleading.

Avoid photorealistic AI recreations of real protests, disasters, historical events, real people, political scenes, social conditions or locations when readers could mistake them for evidence. Don't use generative fill to alter a factual image.

This is deliberately stricter than some publishers' policies, consistent with Viraaasat's documentary integrity promise. Reuters and AP maintain particularly strict standards around AI-generated or altered news imagery. (Sources 1, 2, 4, 5)

## 10. AI audio and voice

### Permitted with review
- Transcription drafts
- Noise cleanup only when it doesn't distort evidence
- Sound-design concepts for fictional/abstract sequences
- Language translation for transcript drafts
- Pronunciation assistance
- Synthetic narration for clearly identified illustrative content, if editorially justified

### Not permitted
- Cloning a real person's voice without consent and clearly justified authorization.
- Creating testimony or ambient sounds that imply an event was recorded when it wasn't.
- Altering documentary audio to change its factual meaning.
- Presenting synthetic narration as the real speaker.
- Creating fake archival audio.

For Viraaasat, original field recording and real voices remain preferred. Synthetic audio should be labeled where it materially affects audience interpretation.

## 11. AI-assisted translation and localization

AI may produce a first draft, but language quality and cultural context require qualified human review.

### Workflow
1. Original language story finalized.
2. Translation draft assisted by AI, if approved.
3. Human translator/editor revises for naturalness, idiom and cultural context.
4. Claims, numbers, names, quotes and source links rechecked.
5. Sensitive terms reviewed.
6. Typography and layout tested in actual target script.
7. Translation status and update date recorded.

Do not automatically translate politically sensitive, regional, caste, religious, community or historical terminology without context review.

## 12. AI-generated data analysis

AI may help:
- Explain data fields
- Suggest analysis methods
- Generate code
- Find possible outliers
- Draft chart captions
- Check formulas
- Suggest alternative visualizations

But it must not:
- Invent missing data
- silently change assumptions
- select only data that supports a narrative
- manufacture statistical significance
- claim correlation as causation
- obscure uncertainty

### Required checks
- Source and provenance
- Data scope
- Definitions
- Missingness
- Cleaning/transformation log
- Calculation validation
- Sample size
- Uncertainty
- Chart scale and annotation
- Reproducibility

A human must reproduce or validate the results using the source dataset or a trusted calculation workflow.

## 13. AI disclosure: when should readers know?

Not every minor AI-assisted grammar correction requires a reader-facing disclosure. But material use that affects content, evidence, interpretation, representation, translation or narration should be transparent.

### Proposed disclosure levels

**Level 0 — Routine assistance**
Spellcheck, formatting, code suggestions, brainstorms and minor copyediting. No individual story disclosure ordinarily needed.

**Level 1 — Significant workflow assistance**
AI helped summarize source material, translate a substantial passage, analyze data or materially shape story elements. Disclosure in a short methodology note may be appropriate.

**Level 2 — Audience-facing generated material**
AI-generated illustrations, synthetic audio, significant AI-generated text or simulations. Clearly label at the point of use, explain purpose and limitations.

**Level 3 — AI is the subject of the story**
Clearly identify generated elements, prompt/method context as appropriate, and distinguish synthetic output from authentic evidence.

### Example note

> **AI assistance:** AI tools were used to help organize and summarize research material and assist with drafting. All factual claims, sources, interpretations and final text were reviewed and verified by the Viraaasat editorial team.

Only use this wording if it accurately describes that story's workflow. Do not apply a blanket statement to stories where it isn't true.

## 14. The Viraaasat AI workflow

### Recommended weekly pipeline

**Step 1 — Story brief (human)**
Topic, central question, scope, editorial purpose and initial hypotheses.

**Step 2 — Research planning (AI-assisted)**
Search terms, source categories, questions, opposing interpretations and gaps.

**Step 3 — Research (human-led)**
Collect original documents, credible reporting, datasets, interviews and evidence.

**Step 4 — Source organization (AI-assisted)**
Summarize documents, extract structured notes, organize timelines. Verify each against the original.

**Step 5 — Narrative outline (human + AI critique)**
Humans choose story structure. AI can critique pacing, clarity, missing context and potential bias.

**Step 6 — Draft (human-led, AI-assisted where appropriate)**
Human writes or substantially authors the narrative. AI helps edit, simplify, question and polish.

**Step 7 — Fact-check (human)**
Verify all claims, citations, figures, dates, quotations, captions and interpretations.

**Step 8 — Visual and interaction design (human-led)**
AI can brainstorm/critique. Actual visuals are sourced, commissioned, designed or generated under the policy.

**Step 9 — Build and QA (human + coding copilot)**
AI assists development; humans inspect code, run tests, evaluate accessibility, responsive behavior and security.

**Step 10 — Publish (human approval)**
No automated publishing without explicit approval.

Maintain a lightweight inventory for the tools actually used.

| Tool | Purpose | Data allowed | Risk | Owner/review |
|---|---|---|---|
| General AI assistant | Research planning, writing critique | Public sources / approved non-sensitive notes | Hallucination, bias | Human verifies |
| Coding assistant | Implementation, tests, debugging | Project code subject to tool policy | Security, unreviewed code | Developer reviews |
| Image generation | Conceptual/illustrative assets | Public creative brief | Misrepresentation | Editorial review |
| Translation tool | Translation drafts | Published/approved text | Nuance/context errors | Fluent reviewer |
| Transcription | Interview/transcript draft | Consent-approved recordings only | Accuracy/privacy | Audio checked |
| Data analysis AI | Code and analysis assistance | Public/approved datasets | Formula/data errors | Reproducible check |

Review the inventory when a tool is added, a major capability changes, or data handling changes.

## 16. Prompting standard for high-risk tasks

For research, analysis and sensitive work, prompts should include:

- Task and scope
- Source material or authoritative references
- What the AI must not assume
- Required output format
- Uncertainty instruction
- Verification checklist
- Explicit request to distinguish source evidence from inference

### Example research prompt

```text
Help organize this research question into a source-finding plan.

Do not invent citations, quotations, statistics, dates, or facts.
Separate:
1. Questions to investigate
2. Primary sources to seek
3. Secondary sources to seek
4. Potential competing interpretations
5. Claims that require verification
6. Gaps and uncertainty

Do not claim that any source has been read unless it is provided
or you can actually access and inspect it.
```

### Example fact-check prompt

```text
Audit this draft against the sources I provide.

For each factual claim:
- Quote the claim briefly
- Identify the supporting source passage
- Classify as supported / partially supported / unsupported /
  contradicted / requires more evidence
- Note scope, caveats and possible ambiguity

Do not use outside knowledge to silently fill source gaps.
Do not fabricate citations. If the evidence is insufficient, say so.
```

AI can help structure the audit, but it cannot certify it.

## 17. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hallucinated citations | Open and verify every source |
| Bias or framing | Human editorial review and competing viewpoints |
| Homogenized voice | Human authorship and style guide |
| Confidential data leakage | Approved tools and input policy |
| Copyright/IP concerns | Avoid uploading restricted content; rights review |
| Fake documentary media | Strict labeling and no synthetic evidence |
| Incorrect translation | Human fluent review |
| AI code vulnerabilities | Diff review, tests, security checks |
| Overdependence | Team maintains skills and understanding |
| Misleading disclosure | Story-specific, accurate disclosure |
| Automation errors | Human approval gates |
| Tool drift | Periodic policy/tool review |

---

## 18. A compact AI policy for Viraaasat

> AI tools may assist our research, development, analysis, design and editorial workflows. They do not replace human reporting, sourcing, verification, interpretation, authorship, editorial judgment or accountability. AI-generated claims and citations are treated as unverified until independently checked. We do not fabricate interviews, quotations, evidence or documentary media. Synthetic visuals and audio are never presented as authentic records. We protect confidential information, review AI-assisted code and disclose material AI use when it affects audience understanding. Humans remain responsible for everything Viraaasat publishes.

## Final takeaway

**AI should increase the capacity of our three-person team—not reduce our responsibility.**

Use it to move faster through research, organize evidence, test ideas, write code, find weaknesses and explore possibilities. But the source of truth is the source itself; the documentary image is the real image; the editorial judgment is ours; and every story carries our responsibility.
