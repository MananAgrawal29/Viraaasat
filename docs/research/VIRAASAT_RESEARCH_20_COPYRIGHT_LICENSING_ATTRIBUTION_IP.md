# Viraaasat Research 20
## Copyright, Licensing, Attribution & Intellectual Property

**Mission:** Establish a practical asset-clearance system for Viraaasat's documentary/editorial website: photography, archival material, video, audio, music, fonts, maps, datasets, software and third-party embeds.

**Research date:** November 22, 2026

> Operational guidance, not legal advice. Laws, licenses, exceptions and platform terms vary by jurisdiction and by use. For unclear or high-risk assets, seek qualified legal advice or use a cleared alternative.

---

## 1. Viraaasat's rights philosophy

Rights clearance is part of the editorial workflow—not a last-minute checkbox. Every published asset must have a traceable source and a documented permission or license basis, plus any required attribution and restrictions.

### Four questions before publication
1. **What is it?** Photo, video, audio, font, map, dataset, code, illustration, etc.
2. **Who controls the relevant rights?** Creator, publisher, institution, rights-holder, licensee or another party.
3. **What permits our specific use?** License, written permission, verified public-domain status or an applicable legal exception.
4. **What conditions apply?** Attribution, link, modifications, commercial restrictions, expiry, consent, or other conditions.

### Not proof of permission
- Being downloadable, publicly accessible or found through search.
- Appearing on social media, YouTube, Pinterest or a repost.
- Having no copyright notice.
- Being old, historical, educational, newsworthy or government-hosted.
- Being credited, cropped, low-resolution or embedded.
- Being labelled “free” without a license that covers our use.

**Attribution does not substitute for permission.**

## 2. Asset-specific clearance

### Documentary photography
Preferred: team-created/commissioned imagery with appropriate agreements; licensed editorial images; item-level CC works; verified public-domain material; rights-cleared institutional collections.

Record photographer, original source page, license/permission, verification date, credit, editing rights, consent/privacy notes and story placement. Do not materially alter documentary images to fabricate context. Ordinary technical resizing or crop/color corrections must remain truthful and license-compliant.

### Historical and archival images
“Archive” does not mean “copyright-free.” Check creator, date, item-level rights statement, copyright term, scan/reproduction rights, collection terms, intended online use and credit. Label colorization, restoration, reconstruction or illustration clearly when material changes could affect interpretation.

### Video and film clips
Rights may include film/video, composition, sound recording, performer/speaker and third-party material. Do not assume short duration automatically makes a clip fair use/fair dealing. Use licensed, commissioned, permitted or public-domain footage with verified status; otherwise link to the original where appropriate, or replace it.

### Music
Often at least two rights exist: the **composition** (music/lyrics) and the **sound recording/master**. Clearance for one may not clear the other. Commissioned music should have a written agreement covering website, distribution, territories, term, promotion and ownership/license. Check exact terms for CC tracks and public-domain compositions; a modern recording may remain protected.

### Interviews and field recordings
Obtain informed permission suitable for recording, publication, excerpts, transcript, translation and future reuse. Consider privacy, safety, anonymity and withdrawal terms. Preserve originals and document edits that could affect meaning. Field recordings should be authentic or licensed—not fabricated and presented as location evidence.

### Fonts
Font files are software. Verify the specific files and license allow web embedding/self-hosting and any redistribution or modification. Retain license and notice files. For Viraaasat's locked font system—Cormorant Garamond, Manrope and IBM Plex Mono—verify the exact source and license for each file actually downloaded; family names alone are not clearance.

### Maps and geospatial data
Clear each layer separately: basemap/tiles, styles, satellite imagery, terrain/DEM, boundaries, labels and datasets. Free APIs may still restrict caching, redistribution, screenshots or tile use. Record provider, layer, terms, attribution and version/date.

### Datasets and charts
Open data may still have attribution, redistribution, privacy or derivative-use conditions. Record publisher, dataset/version, access date, definitions, scope, units, transformations, calculations, missingness, limitations and attribution. Verify the chart independently against the dataset.

### Software and code
Track dependencies, licenses and notices. Review license and maintenance before adding packages; preserve notices as required. Avoid copying substantial code from sources with unclear terms. Viraaasat's code license does not automatically cover assets bundled in the repo.

### AI-generated assets
AI outputs are not automatically copyright-free, exclusive, cleared or legally safe. Review tool terms, privacy, likeness/trademark concerns and provenance. Viraaasat may use clearly labeled conceptual illustration where justified, but **never present photorealistic synthetic images as documentary evidence**. No fake archival images, synthetic testimony or misleading depictions of real people/events/places.

## 3. Creative Commons: check the exact license

Creative Commons is a family of licenses, not a single blanket permission. Identify the exact license and version, creator, source and conditions.

| License | Meaning | Viraaasat action |
|---|---|---|
| CC BY | Attribution; reuse/adaptation, including commercial use, allowed under terms | Usually viable if conditions followed |
| CC BY-SA | Attribution; adaptations under same license | Check compatibility and obligations |
| CC BY-ND | Attribution; no sharing adaptations | Avoid modifications; review crops/derivatives carefully |
| CC BY-NC | Attribution; noncommercial only | Evaluate intended use carefully; don't assume a project is noncommercial |
| CC BY-NC-SA | Noncommercial + share alike | Extra compatibility review |
| CC BY-NC-ND | Noncommercial + no derivatives | Narrow permission; review before use |
| CC0 | Public-domain dedication/waiver tool, subject to legal limits | Still record provenance and consider other rights |

Creative Commons licenses can be irrevocable for compliant uses; confirm exact version and legal code. The NC, ND and SA elements have real consequences, not decorative badges. [CC official license overview](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/use-remix/cc-licenses/) and [FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/).

### Attribution template
> **[Asset title]** — [Creator], via [Source]. Licensed under [License/version]. [Link]. [Modification note, if applicable].

Follow the asset's exact attribution requirements; include creator/title (when supplied), source, license link, notice and modification indication where required.

## 4. India-specific caution

India's Copyright Act, 1957 (as amended) is the core copyright framework. Copyright can arise automatically when qualifying work is created; registration is not generally required for copyright to exist. The official IP India FAQ explains that copyright protects original expression and not ideas or facts as such. See [IP India FAQ](https://ipindia.gov.in/pages/copyright/learn/faqs) and [Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov.in/).

Do not treat “educational,” “research,” “news,” “nonprofit” or “public interest” as automatic permission. Indian statutory exceptions are not identical to US fair use—do not import US assumptions. Public-domain calculations depend on the work type, author, publication/creation dates, jurisdiction and other facts. Government works, archives, commissioned works, photographs, films and recordings may have different rules. Other rights (privacy, publicity/personality, confidentiality, defamation, contracts and moral rights) may remain even where copyright permission exists.

For disputed, sensitive, commercially important or potentially infringing material, consult qualified Indian counsel.

## 5. Public domain: document your determination

Record creator, work category, date, jurisdiction, term calculation/reasoning, source supporting the determination, separate rights in reproduction/scan/edition/recording, collection terms, and date checked. Institutional policies are not universal: verify each item's rights statement.

## 6. Permission-request process

1. Identify the rights-holder or authorized representative.
2. Explain Viraaasat, story context and proposed placement.
3. Specify asset, crop/edit, web publication, promotional use, duration and territory.
4. Request written permission and required credit.
5. Clarify commercial/monetization, archive, future reuse, expiry and restrictions.
6. Save the reply and permission evidence in the rights register.
7. Confirm clearance before publishing.

### Email template
**Subject:** Permission request to use [asset] in Viraaasat editorial story

Hello [Name],

We are creating Viraaasat, an independent storytelling project exploring India through evidence-driven, interactive editorial stories.

We would like to request permission to use **[specific asset]** in our story about **[topic]** on our website, with a contextual caption and credit to **[creator/rights-holder]**.

Could you confirm whether this use is permitted and share any conditions for attribution, editing/cropping, duration, territory or promotional sharing?

Thank you,\nViraaasat editorial team

## 7. Asset rights register (required)

Maintain one shared ledger for all assets.

| Field | What to store |
|---|---|
| Asset ID / Story ID | Unique IDs and story placement |
| File/path + type | Filename, photo/video/audio/font/map/data/code |
| Title/description | Work name or clear description |
| Creator/rights-holder | Name/organization |
| Original source URL | Item-level page, not only homepage |
| License/permission basis | Exact license/version or written permission/public-domain rationale |
| License URL/evidence | Terms link and saved evidence/reply where possible |
| Credit line | Exact required wording |
| Modifications | Crop, color correction, edit, translation, etc. |
| Consent/privacy | Release, restrictions, safety/anonymity notes |
| Commercial/territory/expiry | Limits and review date |
| Verified by/date | Team member and verification date |
| Status/notes | Restrictions, questions and review status |

**Status:** `Needed → Found → Source verified → Rights verified → Prepared → Placed → QA approved`

Uncleared assets do not publish without a documented, explicitly approved legal/editorial exception.

## 8. Preferred sourcing hierarchy

**Tier 1:** Team-created, commissioned work with clear agreements, direct permission.

**Tier 2:** Item-level public-domain, open-access, verified Creative Commons, or licensed assets.

**Tier 3—conditional:** Government, museum/archive, press/news, social-media, stock, video-platform or institution assets; review item/service-specific terms.

**Avoid unless cleared:** Random search-image results, Pinterest reposts, uncredited social posts, unknown provenance, stock previews/watermarks and assets with unclear AI origin.

Discovery services (not automatic clearance): Wikimedia Commons, Openverse, Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, NASA/government collections, Internet Archive, Google Fonts, OpenStreetMap. Search filters do not replace item-level verification.

### Maps: specific reminder
OpenStreetMap data is distributed under the ODbL and public use of produced works requires appropriate attribution. The attribution form depends on the map/database/product. Review OSM Foundation guidance and the legal terms; other tile providers may have separate terms. [OSM Attribution Guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Attribution_Guidelines)

## 9. Credit and placement

Credits should be readable, accessible and near the asset where practicable, or in a clearly linked credit/source area if the license permits and association remains obvious. Don't hide required attribution or make it tiny.

**Photo example:**
> Caption: A worker sorts harvested cotton in Maharashtra, October 2025.\nPhoto: [Creator] / [Source] · [License]

**Chart example:**
> Source: [Publisher], [dataset/version], accessed [date]. Viraaasat calculations; methodology and limitations in notes.

**Archive example:**
> Collection: [Institution/item]. Original dated [date]. Reproduction supplied by [source]. Rights status: [verified status].

## 10. Rights workflow for every weekly story

```text
Story brief
    ↓
Asset list + visual brief
    ↓
Find / commission / create
    ↓
Verify original source
    ↓
Check license, permission or legal basis
    ↓
Record credit, restrictions, edits, consent
    ↓
Optimize + place
    ↓
Editorial and rights QA
    ↓
Publish
```

### Weekly checklist
- [ ] Every asset has a traceable source.
- [ ] Item-level license/permission basis is checked.
- [ ] Attribution recorded and displayed correctly.
- [ ] Modifications are allowed and documented.
- [ ] Privacy/consent/safety reviewed when relevant.
- [ ] Map, data, font and software terms checked.
- [ ] Restrictions, expiry and territory noted.
- [ ] Rights evidence stored.
- [ ] Asset context and authenticity preserved.
- [ ] Mobile and desktop display QA complete.

## 11. When we cannot clear an asset

Don't weaken the story or take the risk. Alternatives:
- Photograph it ourselves.
- Commission a photographer/illustrator.
- Use a cleared contextual substitute.
- Make a diagram, map or data visualization from permitted sources.
- Use typography, annotations, archival text or a non-photorealistic labeled illustration.
- Deliberately leave visual space.

A story can still be compelling without a hero image.

## 12. Public rights promise (proposed)

> **Respect for the work and the people behind it.** We aim to use media we created, commissioned, licensed or verified as reusable. We credit creators and respect licensing conditions. We do not present synthetic or materially altered media as documentary evidence. When provenance or rights are unclear, we seek permission, use an alternative or leave it out.

This should be published only once it reflects our actual practice.

## Final takeaway

**If we can't establish where an asset came from and why we can use it, it doesn't belong in the story yet.** Viraaasat's trust depends not only on factual accuracy, but also on respecting creators, subjects, communities and the evidence that makes our stories possible.
