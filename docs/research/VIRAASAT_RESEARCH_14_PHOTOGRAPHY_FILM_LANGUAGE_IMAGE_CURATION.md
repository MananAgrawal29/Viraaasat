# Viraaasat Research 14
## Photography, Film Language, Image Curation & Visual Evidence

**Mission:** Establish a visual-image philosophy and practical image workflow for Viraaasat—documentary photography, film language, sourcing/licensing, image sequencing, historical material, and generative AI boundaries.

**Editorial north star:** Images should feel observed, not manufactured. They may be cinematic and expressive, but never create a false impression of real people, places, events, or evidence.

---

## 1. The visual identity in one sentence

**Viraaasat's imagery should feel like an intimate documentary film frame from a country too complex to reduce to a single image.**

Our image language:
- Observational rather than staged
- Cinematic but not artificially dramatic
- Textural, human, and specific
- Diverse in region, class, language, age, environment and everyday life
- Thoughtful about dignity and context
- Aware of archival history, while clearly distinguishing archival and contemporary images
- Avoidant of cliché tourism imagery and stereotypes

The aim is not to make every image look like a film still. The aim is to make images **belong to the story**.

---

## 2. Documentary visual language: how photographs tell stories

Photography is not only decoration. A sequence of images can reveal a story through distance, detail, contrast, repetition and change.

### The six useful shot families

| Shot family | Story function | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Establishing / wide | Place and scale | City skyline, landscape, crowd, infrastructure |
| Environmental portrait | A person in their context | Worker at workplace, artisan in workshop, resident in neighborhood |
| Medium / action | What is happening | Hands at work, daily activity, movement |
| Detail / close-up | Texture, evidence, emotional intimacy | Tools, objects, surfaces, documents, food, machinery |
| Archive / historical | Time and memory | Historic photograph, map, document, artifact |
| Contrast / juxtaposition | Reveal difference or connection | Old/new, rural/urban, scale/detail, before/after—only if comparison is valid |

### A simple visual rhythm
A strong editorial sequence often alternates scale and purpose rather than repeating similar images:

**Wide → human → detail → context → contrast → pause**

This is a flexible sequence, not a formula. The story determines what is needed.

## 3. Film language for the web

Use cinematic language with editorial restraint.

### Composition
- **Wide frame:** establish scale, geography or systems.
- **Medium frame:** make a subject and its surroundings legible.
- **Close-up:** create intimacy or reveal detail.
- **Negative space:** allow typography and quiet moments.
- **Layering:** foreground/background can show depth or relationships.
- **Leading lines:** roads, rails, rivers, architecture can guide the eye.
- **Symmetry:** useful for institutions, architecture or ritual—but avoid making it a default.
- **Frame within frame:** doorways, windows, arches, vehicles, boundaries.

### Camera and perspective (when planning commissioned photography)
- Wide lenses emphasize space and proximity; use carefully to avoid distortion.
- Normal focal lengths often feel observational.
- Longer lenses compress distance and may isolate subjects—be aware that this changes context.
- Camera height matters: eye-level can feel present; high/low angles may imply power or vulnerability.
- Do not use perspective or crop to imply a relationship, scale or event that is not accurate.

### Light
- Natural available light is a strong default.
- Soft overcast light is useful for portraits and texture.
- Hard light can reveal architectural geometry and heat.
- Backlight can create silhouette, but ensure important evidence is not hidden.
- Night images should preserve enough context to avoid turning a place into spectacle.

### Color and grading
- Maintain restrained, consistent processing.
- Preserve realistic skin tones and relevant environmental color.
- Avoid aggressive saturation, heavy teal/orange grading, crushed shadows or color shifts that materially change interpretation.
- Story-specific palette may guide UI and graphics, but should not dictate false photographic color.

### Sound and film
- Ambient sound can add presence in story scenes: traffic, water, workshops, train stations, rain, crowd texture.
- Music should not emotionally manipulate readers into sympathy, suspicion, ridicule or a predetermined verdict.
- Use sound intentionally, with user control, captions/transcripts and a quiet default where appropriate.

## 4. Documentary, illustrative, archive and reconstruction — label clearly

Every visual in the editorial system should have a declared type.

### Documentary photograph/video
A real scene recorded by a camera. Caption with accurate context, date/location when known, creator/credit, and any necessary safety/consent considerations.

### Archive
Historical image, film, map, document, artwork or recording. Identify the date/period, creator/collection, source and relevant rights. If date is uncertain, say so.

### Illustration
An authored visual that explains or expresses an idea. It should not be mistaken for documentary evidence.

### Data visualization / graphic
A designed representation of evidence, not a photograph. Include source, labels, units and methodology as appropriate.

### Reconstruction / simulation
A model of a past, inaccessible, hypothetical or technical scene. Label it visibly as reconstruction, simulation or illustrative model. Explain what is based on evidence and what is interpretive.

### Generative AI imagery
Synthetic imagery. Viraaasat should never present it as a real photograph, real event, authentic archival image, or eyewitness evidence. Use conspicuous labeling and keep it outside documentary evidence.

## 5. Image authenticity and editing policy

Use the Associated Press, Reuters, PBS FRONTLINE, UN Photo and professional visual journalism standards as strong reference points for preserving image meaning.

### Routine adjustments generally acceptable for documentary images
- Crop for responsive framing, provided context isn't misleading
- Exposure and white-balance correction
- Modest contrast, tone and color correction
- Dust/scratch cleanup on scanned photographs when it restores the image
- Grayscale conversion, when disclosed or contextually clear and not deceptive
- Resizing, compression and sharpening appropriate for display

### Not acceptable for documentary images
- Adding, removing or moving objects, people, signs or environmental details
- Generative fill that changes the factual scene
- Altering facial expressions, bodies, clothing or other identity cues
- Replacing backgrounds or materially changing context
- Manipulating weather, lighting or atmosphere to suggest something that did not happen
- Cropping that falsely implies proximity, association or action
- Editing that obscures, exaggerates or changes the meaning of evidence
- Presenting a composite as an authentic photograph

If an image is altered materially for an explanatory graphic, make the graphic unmistakably designed rather than a deceptive photo.

**Source basis:** AP says imagery must remain accurate and limits photo adjustments to changes that preserve authentic content; Reuters prohibits altering still images or video beyond normal editorial preparation; UN Photo disallows manipulations that alter image content or meaning. See Sources.

## 6. AI imagery policy — proposed Viraaasat rules

AI may be useful for design experimentation, conceptual illustration or abstract visual systems. It is not a substitute for real evidence.

### Permitted with clear labeling and review
- Abstract, non-photorealistic conceptual illustrations
- Clearly stylized diagrams and symbolic artwork
- Decorative backgrounds that do not claim to represent a real location or event
- UI prototyping and design exploration
- Non-documentary visual metaphors

### Avoid or prohibit in editorial storytelling
- Photorealistic generated images of actual events, people, places, disasters, protests, conflict, suffering or historical scenes presented as documentary
- Synthetic “archive” images made to look like authentic historical photographs
- AI-generated depictions that could reinforce harmful stereotypes about communities
- Altering real documentary photographs to add/remove/modify evidence
- Cloning voices of real people
- AI-generated visuals used to fill gaps in evidence without conspicuous disclosure

### Viraaasat's stronger default
**Do not use photorealistic AI imagery inside the documentary story body.** If there is no authentic image, use:
1. A real but clearly contextualized related image (caption explicitly states it is illustrative and not from the specific event).
2. A designed graphic, map, timeline or diagram.
3. A non-photorealistic illustration clearly labeled.
4. Typography, color, texture, or an intentional absence of image.

This policy is deliberately stricter than general creative marketing practice because Viraaasat's format may blur journalism, visual design and cinematic presentation.

## 7. Image sourcing and rights

Image availability does not mean reuse is allowed.

### Recommended sourcing ladder
1. **Commission original photography** for key scenes, if feasible.
2. **Public-domain or open-access collections** where item-level rights and conditions are verified.
3. **Creative Commons** assets after checking the exact license, creator, attribution, modification and commercial-use terms.
4. **Licensed editorial/stock libraries** with the appropriate use rights.
5. **Press and institutional images** only when their terms clearly permit the intended use.
6. **Social media or user-generated content** only after verifying authenticity, obtaining permission/license as required, and assessing source safety.

### Rights register — required for each asset
- Asset ID and file name
- Story slug and placement
- Creator/photographer
- Original source URL or collection
- Original caption/description
- Capture date and location (if known)
- License/permission and terms
- Required credit
- Expiry/territorial/format restrictions (if any)
- Whether cropping or modification is permitted
- Consent/safety considerations
- Verification notes
- Reviewer and approval date

### License checks
- Is the asset licensed for website publication?
- Is commercial use allowed? (Assume the project could later be monetized.)
- Are adaptations/crops allowed?
- Is attribution required, and in what form?
- Are there restrictions on people, trademarks, artworks, architecture or other third-party rights?
- Is a model/property release required for the intended use?
- Does the license apply to this specific asset, not just the website or collection generally?

Do not rely on “found on Google,” “free download,” or an unclear license label.

## 8. Image discovery: search with intent

Avoid searching only broad phrases like “India beautiful” or “Indian culture.”

Search in layers:

**Subject + action + place + time/context + image type**

Examples:
- `potter shaping clay workshop Rajasthan documentary photo`
- `Mumbai suburban railway commuters evening photography`
- `Indian groundwater farmers borewell documentary`
- `archival textile mill workers India 1980 photograph`
- `river floodplain satellite image India dataset`
- `handloom weaver portrait working environment Odisha`

Search for a visual **role**, not merely a subject:
- Establishing scene
- Human perspective
- Detail/evidence
- Historical context
- Contradiction
- Transition
- Quiet ending

This prevents repetitive imagery and helps identify the images the narrative actually needs.

## 9. Visual curation: building a story's image palette

For every story, create a small visual brief before searching.

### Visual brief
- **Story question:** What is the central question?
- **Emotional register:** Curious, uneasy, hopeful, contemplative, urgent, intimate?
- **Visual environment:** Urban, rural, industrial, archival, domestic, natural, institutional?
- **Palette:** What color, light and texture are naturally present?
- **Human scale:** Who or what gives the story a lived perspective?
- **Evidence imagery:** What visual can directly support the narrative?
- **Contrast:** Which visual juxtaposition is valid and meaningful?
- **Avoid:** Clichés, stereotypes, generic stock, sensationalism, false visual associations.

### Curated image roles
A story's image set may include:
- 1 hero image or opening scene
- 2–4 establishing images
- 3–6 human/action images
- 2–5 detail images
- 1–3 archival images or documents if relevant
- 1–2 transition/quiet images

These are planning ranges, not quotas. Some stories need fewer images, some need maps, charts, video or illustrations instead.

## 10. Story-specific image sequences

### History
- Artifact or archival document
- Wide historical environment
- Detail of material evidence
- Map showing geography
- Contemporary place, clearly distinguished from past
- Timeline or reconstructed diagram where needed

### Society
- Environmental portraits
- Everyday action
- Contextual wide frames
- Objects and places that show systems
- Consent-aware, dignity-first depiction

### Geography / climate
- Wide landscapes
- Aerial or satellite view
- Ground-level human experience
- Seasonal or temporal comparisons
- Map and data layers

### Economy / work
- Workplace context
- Tools and process
- Workers (with consent and respect)
- Supply-chain/flow diagrams
- Avoid imagery that glamorizes, caricatures or decontextualizes labour

### Politics / institutions
- Building or institutional environment
- Documents, maps, public records
- People affected by policy
- Avoid generic political imagery that implies a specific event or allegation

### Culture
- Practice/process rather than only spectacle
- Details of material and craft
- Portraits with context
- Everyday contemporary life alongside heritage
- Avoid treating cultures as frozen in time

### Conflict / difficult truths
- Safety, consent, dignity and context first
- Avoid gratuitous imagery
- Don't use unrelated generic images as if they show the event
- Provide content warnings when needed
- Consider whether the visual is necessary to inform the reader

## 11. The image sequence is a narrative

A story should not be an uninterrupted gallery of similarly framed images.

A possible rhythm:
1. **Arrival:** wide image that opens the world
2. **Orientation:** a clear context image, map or graphic
3. **Intimacy:** a person or detail
4. **Evidence:** document, data, object or visual demonstration
5. **Scale shift:** wide or spatial image
6. **Reveal:** juxtaposition, diagram, or decisive visual
7. **Pause:** quiet image, negative space or minimal typography
8. **Exit:** a visual that lingers, rather than an obligatory summary image

Let the narrative guide the sequence. Avoid repeatedly using a dramatic hero image when a quiet, ordinary frame may be more truthful and memorable.

## 12. Hero image selection

A hero image should:
- Be specific to the story
- Carry the subject's context, not just aesthetic beauty
- Work at both desktop and mobile crops
- Have a clear focal point
- Leave room for text when text overlays are needed
- Be authentic and accurately captioned
- Have verified rights
- Avoid sensationalism or stereotypical shorthand

### Review crops
Check at least:
- Wide desktop
- Medium tablet
- Narrow mobile
- Portrait/social crop

If the image only works in one crop, consider a different image or a responsive art-direction approach with alternate authorized crops.

---

## 13. Captions, credits and context

Captions are not optional metadata. They are part of the reporting and reader experience.

A good caption answers what is visible and what the reader needs to understand:
- Who/what is shown?
- Where?
- When?
- What is happening?
- Why is it relevant?
- Is it archival, illustrative or reconstructed?

Credit the creator and source according to rights terms. Do not use captions to make unsupported claims about people shown. Avoid assuming a person’s identity, occupation, community, emotions or intent from appearance alone.

For related/illustrative images, explicitly state when they are not from the specific event or place discussed.

## 14. Image-led layout patterns for Viraaasat

Use images in ways that help the reader think.

- **Full-bleed image:** emotional or spatial opening; use sparingly.
- **Split image/text:** context beside an image, particularly desktop.
- **Editorial image break:** a visual pause between chapters.
- **Image + annotation:** labels point to evidence within a frame.
- **Before/after:** only with equivalent context and accurate dates.
- **Diptych/juxtaposition:** reveal a meaningful contrast without implying false equivalence.
- **Image rail:** multiple frames to show progression or variation.
- **Archive-to-present transition:** clearly date and label each image.
- **Map-to-ground transition:** spatial overview followed by human-scale view.
- **Image with sound:** optional ambient scene, controlled playback.

Motion should never alter the factual content of an image. Animated crops, pans, parallax or dissolves must preserve meaning and should not hide evidence or create false temporal relationships.

## 15. Accessibility and responsive image storytelling

- Provide appropriate alt text; decorative imagery should use empty alt text when implemented in HTML.
- Alt text should describe the image's purpose in context—not repeat the caption verbatim.
- Captions provide context; alt text conveys meaningful visual information to users who cannot see the image.
- Avoid putting essential text only inside an image.
- Ensure captions, credits and overlays are readable and contrast-checked.
- Use responsive images and art direction.
- Reserve image aspect ratio to avoid layout shift.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images; prioritize the actual opening/LCP image.
- Use optimized formats and appropriate resolution.
- Avoid scroll effects that cause nausea or hide information.
- Reduced-motion users should receive a coherent static version.
- Test image loading failures and low bandwidth.

## 16. Proposed Viraaasat visual asset workflow

**Story brief → visual brief → shot/asset list → discovery or commission → rights & authenticity check → edit → caption/credit → responsive crops → placement → QA → publish**

### Asset status
- Needed
- Found
- Authenticity checked
- Rights verified
- Selected
- Edited/optimized
- Captioned and credited
- Placed
- QA approved

No image should enter the final story merely because it looks good. It must have a story purpose, verified provenance and appropriate usage rights.

---

## 17. Recommended policy to add to the project

### Viraaasat Visual Integrity Promise (draft)
> We use images to illuminate reality, not manufacture it. Documentary photographs and footage are not materially manipulated. Archival material, illustration, reconstruction, simulation and generative imagery are identified clearly. We verify image provenance and context, respect rights and permissions, and prioritize dignity and safety. When authentic imagery is unavailable, we prefer a clear graphic, illustration, map, or an intentional absence over a fabricated documentary image.

## Final takeaway

**India should never look like a stock-photo collection on Viraaasat.**

It should look lived in: the details, the contradictions, the light, the people, the places and the textures that reveal something real.

Our images can be cinematic. Our compositions can be deliberate. Our layouts can be experimental.

But the underlying visual evidence must remain honest.
